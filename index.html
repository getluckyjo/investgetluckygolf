Why an insurer can cover it
  • Signed by Golfzon, checked by the insurer. A forged ace fails.
  • Settings locked and fingerprinted. A venue that lowers the difficulty gets refunded, not played.
  • One entry, one shot, and unplayed entries expire after 15 minutes.
  • The hole rotates weekly on the hardest setting, so nobody can practise it.

After the broker's commission, the cover breaks even at one ace in 5,208 swings. Golfzon measures about one in 6,992 across every setting. Santam has agreed in principle; the tiers are bound once priced on Golfzon's own ace data.

06 · The risks

What could go wrong, and what we do about it.

Will Golfzon's bays carry this, and what if Korea says no?

Phase one runs on features Golfzon's bays already have, and the integration demo is built. Most rounds are in Korea, where paid-entry prizes still need clearing; the plan assumes 2027. On the rest of the network alone the round marks at 1.24× and cash would run to Q1 2028; we'd know about Korea during 2027 and cut costs well before then. Trackman is the hedge: add its bays from 2028, without Korea, and the round marks at 1.53×, or 5.27× by 2032.

What if Golfzon doesn't sign?

Then the app carries the plan, on course in South Africa and worldwide. With no Golfzon at all, 2029 revenue is R24.0m, not R51.8m, and the round marks at 1.16× in 2029 and 3.35× by 2032. With every cost held at plan, cash lasts to Q4 2027; we'd hold back the simulator team and take the integration to the next network instead.

What if Golfzon's roll-out runs late?

A year late, the round marks at 1.70× in 2029 and 8.65× by 2032, and cash lasts to Q1 2028 if we cut nothing. Phase one needs no new Golfzon software, which is why it comes first, and a slow start would show in 2027's numbers, in time to hold the hiring back.

Will an insurer cover a simulator ace?

Santam has agreed in principle. After commission the cover breaks even at one ace in 5,208; Golfzon measures about one in 6,992. That's why we play the hardest setting, rotate the hole weekly and cap entries. If the premium lands at 30%, not 24%, it comes out of our share and the round marks at 2.34×. Indwe both sponsors us and places the cover, so a second carrier joins the panel before the simulator tiers scale.

What if the development partner doesn't come in?

Then we raise the R2.8m in cash at the same price, R6.2m for 10% in all, and the plan doesn't change: the work costs the same either way.

Isn't this gambling?

No. It's a skill competition with an insured prize: there's no pot, and no golfer's entry funds another golfer's win. We have a written legal opinion for South Africa and clear each market before we sell there.

Straight answers

Investor FAQ

The questions investors ask first. Anything else, ask me on a call.

What exactly am I buying?

Ordinary shares in Get Lucky Golf Club (Pty) Ltd: R3.4m for 5.5% at R55.8m pre-money, from R1.0m, in Q1 2027. Alongside it, a development partner takes 4.5% for R2.8m of build work at the same price, so the round is 10% in all. No liquidation preference and no ratchet.

What does Ernie get?

5% non-diluting equity as Founding Ambassador, with normal shareholder rights: his 5% shares in any profit we pay out and in any sale, like every other share. No fee, no capital, no guarantees, no liability. His 5% sits inside the R55.8m pre-money, top-up included, so the round still buys its full stake. The full terms are available under NDA.

Why asset-light?

Because the swing runs on golf simulation software, on screens and phones that already exist: Golfzon's bays, ranges that track every ball, and the golfer's own phone on course. We don't buy simulators or screens, so the round builds the platform and the launch, and every new network adds revenue without capex.

What if someone wins R1m, or $100,000?

The insurer pays, and it's the best marketing day of our year. 24% of every entry goes to the insurer as premium, on the same swing that creates the risk.
