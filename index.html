01 · The app

We built it. It's live.

One app runs every swing we sell. Pick a par 3, pick a stake, film the swing. Hole it and the insurer pays the prize, never another golfer. It is live on 101 South African courses and installs from the browser, no app store needed.

  1. Pick a par 3

    101 courses, and every par 3 of 140 m or more on them.

  2. Pick a stake

    R50 to win R25,000, up to R1,000 to win R1m.

  3. Pay

    PayFast today. Apple Pay and Google Pay are built and switch on next.

  4. Film the swing

    A playing partner films it. The clip is fingerprinted the moment it lands, even on weak signal.

  5. Claim

    One evidence pack the insurer can check for itself. Two people sign off every payout.

  • Golf days on their own link

    Every sponsored day gets its own page, and every player gets a free swing. Multi-day trips and dollar prizes too.

  • Insured, not promised

    Prizes are underwritten by Santam and placed by Indwe Risk Services (FSP 3425). No golfer's stake funds another golfer's prize.

  • Built to be audited

    About 800 automated tests, error monitoring, append-only audit logs and fraud rules on every claim.

  • Ready for the bay

    The same app arms a Golfzon bay: scan the QR on the screen, pay, swing. No new app for the golfer to learn.

How a swing works on a Golfzon bay

  1. Scan

    Scan the QR on the bay screen. It arms that bay and no other.

  2. Pay

    Pay in the Get Lucky app. Golfzon loads the challenge hole and locks its settings.

  3. Swing

    One swing. Golfzon signs the shot record the moment the ball stops.

  4. Claim

    An ace opens a claim with the swing video. The insurer checks Golfzon's signature itself.

Why an insurer can cover it

  • Signed by Golfzon, checked by the insurer. A forged ace fails the check.
  • Settings locked and fingerprinted. A venue that lowers the difficulty gets the entry refunded, not played.
  • One entry, one shot. The first shot counts; anything after it is logged and ignored.
  • Unplayed entries expire after 15 minutes and the stake is refunded.
  • The hole rotates weekly, on the hardest setting with no near-cup assist, so it can't be practised.

After the broker's commission, the cover breaks even at one ace in 5,208 swings. Golfzon's measured rate across every setting is about one in 6,992. Santam has agreed in principle to cover simulator swings; the tiers are bound once priced on Golfzon's own ace data.

Straight about the risk

The questions we get asked first.

Will Golfzon's bays carry this, and what if Korea says no?

Phase one needs nothing new from Golfzon. Its proposal runs the challenge on competition features its bays already have, and we built the working integration. Building it into the game goes through Golfzon's product, engineering and legal review, which is why the plan starts at one round in two thousand.

Most of the network's rounds are in Korea, where a paid-entry prize needs clearing. The plan assumes it clears in 2027, so we publish the case without it: on the rest of the network alone, the round marks at 2.17× in 2029. Outside Korea and without Virgin Active, 1.13×, and with every cost held at plan the round lasts to Q1 2029: we would cut costs before then.

Will an insurer cover a simulator ace?

Santam has agreed in principle to cover simulator swings. After the broker's commission the cover breaks even at one ace in 5,208 swings; Golfzon's measured rate is about one in 6,992, which is why the challenge runs on the hardest setting with a rotating hole and capped entries. If the tiers are priced above 24%, the difference comes out of our 46%: at 30%, the round marks at 3.05× in 2029.

Indwe both sponsors us and earns commission placing the cover. The book is built to suit any prize-indemnity underwriter, and a second carrier comes onto the panel before the simulator tiers scale.

Who pays for the bays?

The bay company, not the round. RMB has modelled 90% of each bay's landed cost but has not committed. The bay investors put up the other 10%, a R3m reserve and what the bays need while they ramp: R33.9m between 2027 and 2029, R14.0m of it in 2027.

This is the plan's tightest point. Golfzon wants paying before shipment, so no bay is ordered until both are in place, and Planet Fitness's Q1 2027 date depends on that. If the bay company's raise is slow, the bays wait and our fee waits with them; Get Lucky never pays for a bay.

What if the bays sit empty, or Virgin Active says no?

The plan books a Signature bay for 4.5 of its fifteen hours a day, and the others for less. At 70% of the plan, bay EBITDA covers the debt service 0.84× in 2029 and 1.17× at maturity. The bay investors carry that gap, putting in R57.4m rather than R33.9m, nothing is paid out to Get Lucky before 2032, and the round marks at 2.00× in 2029.

Without Virgin Active, Get Lucky's 2029 revenue is R42.5m and the round marks at 2.16×.

Isn't this gambling?

It is a skill competition with an insured prize. An entry fee to us and a prize paid by our insurer: no golfer's entry funds another's win, so there is no pot and no counterparty. A written legal opinion covers South Africa, and each market is cleared before we sell there, with Golfzon's legal team on its own territories.

Straight answers

Investor FAQ

What exactly am I buying?

Now: a convertible note in Get Lucky Golf Club (Pty) Ltd, R1.0m in total, converting into the Q1 2027 round at a R36m pre-money cap or a 20% discount to its price, whichever is better for you. In Q1 2027: ordinary shares, R14.0m for 20% at R56m pre-money and R70m post, from a R1.0m minimum. The note converts inside the pre-money, so the round buys exactly 20%. Both are in Get Lucky, not the bay company.

Why two companies?

Because the bays and the platform are different investments. A bay is an asset that debt can finance; the platform is a growth business that equity should. With the bays in their own company, your money builds Golfzon and the team instead of buying hardware, Get Lucky carries no bay debt, and the bays are funded by investors who want an asset-backed investment that pays out. Get Lucky still earns 6% of everything the bays take, and half their profit once the bay investors have their capital and 15% a year back.

Why is Virgin Active in the plan if it is not signed?

Because it is the same deal as Planet Fitness, which is signed, at chain scale, and Virgin Active added two simulator bays at a London club in September. We show it as in talks and model it from mid-2027 rather than from the start. Without it, Get Lucky's 2029 revenue is R42.5m and the round marks at 2.16×.

Can I invest in the bay company too?

Yes, separately. The bay investors put up R33.9m between 2027 and 2029 and take their capital and a 15% a year preferred return first; after that the profit splits 50/50 with Get Lucky. On the plan they have it all back by 2030. Those terms are our proposal, final once the bay investors agree them. Ask us for the bay company's pack.

What does Ernie get?

Equity and a share of profit as Founding Ambassador. No fee, no capital, no guarantees and no liability, and his counsel signs off any use of his name. His equity is already in the R56m pre-money; the terms, including how it is treated when we issue shares, are available under NDA.

What if someone wins R1m, or $100,000?

The insurer pays, and it is the best marketing day of the year. Every prize is fully underwritten, with 24% of every entry ceded as premium on the same swing that creates the risk.
